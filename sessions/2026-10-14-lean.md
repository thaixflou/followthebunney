# Session log: 2026-10-14 (lean offer page)

## In two lines
Thais said the audit was over-complicated for a first version: the ICP (founders whose business works but lives on referrals) should see what the first sprint delivers, with a calculator as the hook. We replaced the app with one bilingual page and removed the audit.

## What we did
- Agreed the lean shape: opener, calculator on the person's own numbers, the sprint (five day-28 deliverables), one booking button.
- Built `app/Offer.tsx`, `content/offer.ts` (English and Portuguese, switch button, DRAFT copy) and `lib/referral.ts` with `scripts/test-referral.ts`.
- Calculator: client value, clients won in 12 months, how many from referrals, clients wanted. Shows referral share, gap in clients and what it is worth per year, and the US$2,000 sprint as a share of one client (or how many clients cover it).
- Removed the Airbnb, agency and outbound paths and the audit engine (`Audit.tsx`, `[path]` pages, `lib/engine|paths|types|csv|funnel.ts`, `test-engine.ts`) and the audit-only CSS. All still in git history (commit e7b67da).
- Updated README and CLAUDE.md to match.
- Dev server on 3002 was already running for this folder and hot-reloaded the new page.

## What we decided (details go in decisions.md)
- One page, one ICP, one offer; audit removed.
- Sprint is US$2,000 flat, no 50/50 wording.
- English and Portuguese with a switch button; calculator in US dollars in both.
- Booking link is Calendly.

## What we learned
- Thais's ICP does not buy an audit; they buy the result. The audit is at most a front door to the offer.
- Stale `.next/types` from the dev server can make `tsc` fail after deleting routes; running the build first regenerates them.
